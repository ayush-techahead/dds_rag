Sandy Fowler

For Sandy Fowler, painting is more than just a creative outlet—it’s a way to spread joy. At 63, she has been creating art for as long as she can remember, but in recent years, she’s refined her skills through classes at [The Arc of San Diego’s Sol and Ruth Gerber Family Center](<https://www.arc-sd.com/about/the-sol-and-ruth-gerber-family-center/>) for 14 years. There, she finds purpose in making “people’s hearts happy” with her work.

Since 2022, she has been a part of the Art for All program, learning from local artist Bryttney-Mischele Salvant. Through the program, she has explored new techniques, working with both watercolor and acrylics. “Watercolor is difficult because you have to use it very carefully, but if you use acrylic paint, you can always paint over your mistakes,” she explains.

For several years, The Arc of San Diego has featured Sandy’s paintings on its holiday greeting cards.

For her, the true reward of art is its impact on others. “The reason why I like doing artwork is because it helps me make pictures for other people,” she says. “I like to see their hearts be happy.”

She was excited about the chance to sell her work at the Art for All exhibit—especially because she’s saving for a special occasion. “I’m saving the money for my anniversary in November,” she says. She and her husband, Richard, will celebrate 34 years of marriage. For Sandy, art is more than a talent—it’s a way to connect with others and share kindness. “It just makes me feel grateful that I got a special gift from God.”

The exhibit took place on March 20 at the Bonita Museum & Cultural Center. It featured 13 artists, in celebration of Developmental Disabilities month. All proceeds from artwork sales went directly to the artists.
